# Standalone Simulation Journal

This is the concise ledger of the standalone simulation as it exists now.

## Rules

- Keep this journal synchronized with the current implementation and remove stale descriptions.
- Performance is an overarching requirement. Prefer fixed-size work, shared data, and on-demand diagnostics over per-agent background work.
- Gameplay state belongs in `sim_core`; visualization and camera settings belong in `viewer_raylib`.
- Every walking or running agent must receive stick intent and route its transform through the locomotion mover. Behavior code may not write an agent root transform directly.
- New deterministic inputs must be recorded so an exact tick can be replayed. Rewind is a debug feature and must compile out with `PROPHECY_ENABLE_REWIND=OFF`.
- Every visual debugging element must be named here with its meaning. Unexplained circles, lines, colors, or markers are not allowed.
- Visual changes require inspection of actual rendered viewer captures at representative ticks. Numeric telemetry alone cannot validate pose, gait, orientation, framing, or readability.
- Unreal integration notes stay in `UNREAL_MIGRATION.md`, separate from this standalone journal.
- Hot rebuilds must remain quiet, hidden, and must not take foreground focus.

## Current Simulation

- C++17 deterministic fixed-step core at 30 Hz, matching the accepted locomotion datasets and policies.
- Exactly two default agents: Azure 1 and Crimson 2.
- Each agent owns only a team, deterministic locomotion state, and deterministic stick intent. A small seeded exerciser changes walk/run mode, speed-stick direction/amplitude, and orientation-stick yaw every 55-125 ticks so both movers can be observed. Its root-relative speed-stick angle varies by about +/-20 degrees, intentionally exercising lateral and strafing locomotion.
- Stick direction is root-relative and follows the dataset convention `[sin(angle), cos(angle)]` on the ground plane. Orientation-stick yaw is world-relative.
- The live mover is a native port of `training/slashes2/build_walk_mover_viewer.py`: walk uses its adaptive vector controller, run uses its split acceleration/braking/lateral/reverse response classes, and both use the same yaw motor.
- Directional walk/run speed caps retain the full values measured from the accepted omni-directional datasets.
- Every step publishes authoritative root position, velocity, yaw, response class, and an eight-frame future-root window. The future window is generated by cloning and advancing the same mover state.
- Both agents own equipped swords. A dedicated seeded equipment stream independently toggles each sword between sheathed and drawn every 75-180 ticks without perturbing locomotion RNG.
- Deterministic seed, tick, elapsed time, reset, replay log, exact-tick seek, and return to the live head remain active.
- The current gameplay scope is locomotion only.

## Current Visualization

- One ground plane with a four-meter grid and two full-body animated skeletons.
- Bone segments are thin five-sided cylinders for legibility. No segment is drawn from an artificial ground root to the pelvis.
- Each agent has a compact ground marker showing its displayed locomotion root position and authoritative root yaw.
- Equipped agents wear a dark hip sheath. A sheathed sword shows its grip at the sheath; a drawn sword is aligned from the authored right forearm through the right hand. Equipment does not alter the authored body pose.
- Flying camera: layout-aware ZQSD movement follows the printed letters, with Shift boost, left-drag look, reversed right-drag viewport-plane pan, and wheel dolly.
- Double-clicking an agent enters a close follow camera centered near chest height. Left-drag orbits and the wheel changes follow distance; ZQSD or right-drag exits follow and immediately performs the corresponding free-camera action.
- A single click selects an agent and switches the top-right panel to its locomotion, stick, facing, and equipment state. Enter follows the selected agent; clicking empty ground clears selection.
- Follow tracking runs after fixed-step advancement against the snapshot rendered that frame. Between 30 Hz ticks, the viewer projects root position and authored gait phase from the fixed-step remainder and published velocity. Skeletons, root markers, picking, and follow focus share this presentation transform; telemetry, replay, and simulation state remain exact-tick data.
- Camera-only options: look, pan, flight, and dolly sensitivity. Defaults can be restored and settings can be saved.
- Compact transport: play/pause, one-second rewind, previous tick, next tick, one-second advance, live head, reset, new seed, raw-logic benchmark, speed, FPS, and camera options.
- Keyboard transport: Space toggles play/pause; Left and Right move one tick.
- The top-right panel reports each agent's team, ID, locomotion mode, root speed, and mover response.

## Visual Debugging

- Azure bone color means team Azure.
- Crimson bone color means team Crimson.
- Cream spheres are heads, not state markers.
- The small gold point is the displayed root location: exact on simulation ticks and velocity-projected between them. Its short gold arrow shows authoritative root yaw, not travel direction or stick intent.
- The plane grid is an environmental scale reference, not an agent signal.
- A dark hip scabbard means the agent has a sword equipped. A brown grip at its gold mouth means sheathed; a silver blade and gold guard at the right hand mean drawn.
- Four short white ground-corner marks identify the selected agent. They are a selection indicator, not a range or collision boundary.

## Inspection And Performance

- `GET http://127.0.0.1:17831/snapshot` publishes complete numeric agent state only when requested, including stick intent, future roots, and sword equipment state.
- Telemetry performs no per-frame serialization and can be disabled with `--no-telemetry`.
- `--capture <path> --capture-tick <tick>` records the actual native viewport for visual inspection; `--capture-follow-agent <id>` frames that capture through the same follow camera without synthesizing desktop input.
- `--headless-benchmark` runs the simulation core without opening a window. The in-view CPU button runs the same raw benchmark.
- Authored full-body poses are converted once and loaded as a roughly 309 KB viewer asset. Normal launches do not initialize Python, PyTorch, CUDA, or either checkpoint.

## Verification Contract

- Python/C++ parity over a 360-frame gait-switch trace: zero root-position error, `1.78e-15` maximum velocity error, zero yaw error, and zero response mismatches.
- Same seed and config reproduce all stick inputs, root states, response classes, pose phases, and future roots.
- Replay reproduces the complete locomotion snapshot at an exact requested tick.
- Walk/run speed caps and direction convention are locked by unit tests.
- Rewind-enabled and rewind-disabled configurations must both build and pass tests.
- Representative viewport captures must show upright, readable poses and visible displacement without unexplained visual signals.
