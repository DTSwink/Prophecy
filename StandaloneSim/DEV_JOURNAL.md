# Standalone Simulation Journal

## Current Simulation

- C++17 deterministic fixed-step core at 30 Hz, matching the accepted locomotion datasets and policies.
- Team size is explicit and persistent from zero through 100 per side, including a valid empty zero-versus-zero run. The current saved run is ten blue Heroes versus ten red Villains; committing either count restarts tick zero with the same seed and the applicable saved opening layout.
- Pressing `R` over the bounded ground plane spawns one transient red Villain at the mouse point; `B` spawns one transient blue Hero. Each faces horizontally toward the current camera and starts Normal, equipped, and sheathed. A press adds at most one agent and does nothing over UI, outside the ground bounds, or after that team reaches 100 live agents.
- Transient spawns exist only in the current runtime. They do not change configured team counts, opening transforms, or replay input; checkpoint capture is skipped while they exist, so reset or any rewind/seek reconstructs the recorded timeline without the spawned agents or their consequences.
- A persisted opening layout stores ground translation and root facing separately for Heroes and Villains and overrides generated placement on reset, seed change, mode change, team-count restart, and app launch. When a requested team is larger than its saved transforms, extras are placed deterministically from the active seed inside the saved layout's overall axis-aligned ground bounds; the expanded layout is immediately persisted. Reducing a team uses the first saved transforms without deleting the larger saved layout.
- Without a saved layout, the teams start in centered lines exactly five meters apart with three-meter lane spacing. The default Hero occupies `x=-2.5, z=0`; Villains occupy `x=+2.5` at `z=-3,0,+3`. All face the opposing line with zero stick input.
- Every agent starts combat-aware but Idle, stationary, equipped with a sheathed sword, with no target and its initial head search already complete. There is no global delayed Attack event. On a later fixed tick, current sight can wake the agent and acquire its nearest perceived standing opponent with stable lower-ID tie breaking. An unrecognized opponent behind the head vision cone does not wake the agent through proximity; only an in-range enemy locomotion sound that passes the current active-threat direction rule can begin an investigation, and the opponent must then enter head vision before becoming a recognized threat.
- Attack behavior first completes the timed sword draw. Ordinary visual acquisition draws in place. When a sound investigation visually confirms that its source is currently running toward the listener, a sheathed listener instead supplies a full Walk stick directly away from that source while orienting toward it and completing the same timed draw; the retreat flag clears when the sword is fully drawn. Existing attack behavior then supplies a full forward Run stick and target-facing orientation stick through the normal locomotion mover until measured root distance reaches the exact 1.25-meter attack gate. There is no invented close-range slowdown, and behavior never writes root transforms directly.
- Stick direction is root-relative and follows the dataset convention `[sin(angle), cos(angle)]` on the ground plane. Orientation-stick yaw is world-relative.
- The live mover is a native port of `training/slashes2/build_walk_mover_viewer.py`: walk uses its adaptive vector controller, run uses its split acceleration/braking/lateral/reverse response classes, and both use the same yaw motor.
- Directional walk/run speed caps retain the full values measured from the accepted omni-directional datasets.
- Every step publishes authoritative root position, velocity, yaw, response class, and an eight-frame future-root window. The future window is generated by cloning and advancing the same mover state.
- Head look is authoritative fixed-step state with no roll. Outside combat, root speed at or above exactly 0.5 m/s selects root-velocity direction; slower movement selects root heading. In Attack behavior a living target overrides both and selects that opponent's head anchor.
- Horizontal look yaw is clamped to `+/-120` degrees relative to the root, representing combined head-and-torso turning; pitch remains clamped to `+/-80` degrees. Their shared maximum local turn speed is persistent and hot-applied without restarting the run, defaulting to 360 degrees per second. The current lightweight combat pitch uses the accepted rig's 1.495376-meter reference head height transformed by the existing grounded-state pose.
- All agents start equipped with sheathed swords. At Attack activation each draws before running. Sheathe and unsheathe use the same central timed-action path at exactly 1.7 times their former speed: configured durations are `1.6 / 1.7` and `1.4 / 1.7` seconds, then quantized to the 30 Hz fixed step.
- Sticks are persistent bounded world entities with stable IDs, ground transform, and optional holder. An explicit pickup request first completes the existing sword-sheathe action when necessary, then performs a 1.0-second right-hand pickup; explicit drop takes 0.1 seconds and leaves that same stick at the holder's root for another agent. The equipped sword remains owned and sheathed while a stick is held. Spawn, pickup, and drop are deterministic replay commands. No training-camp source or autonomous pickup/drop behavior exists yet.
- Against a standing target, each seeded attack choice with a usable held weapon is 90 percent weapon and 10 percent melee. A stick uses the authored sword attack clips but carries explicit `Stick` weapon identity; a real sword remains in hand during standing-target melee. Against any living target on the floor (`Agonising`, `PassedOut`, or `Crawling`), a stick holder with a usable equipped sword first completes the timed stick drop, then draws and uses the real sword; if no usable sword exists, only an authored left/right kick is eligible. The selected animation is uniformly random within the eligible authored clips, and action duration is the selected clip's source duration.
- An agent cannot begin another action while attacking. Non-attack actions may overlap; specifically, parry/dodge reactions have independent runtime state and can overlap an existing non-attack action.
- A defender only reacts when the attacker's root is inside its current head-centered vision cone. An unseen attack gets the passive `hit` outcome. A defender already attacking likewise cannot parry or dodge. All attacks launched on the same fixed step choose their defense outcome from the same immutable start-tick batch. Otherwise 20 percent of incoming attacks choose `hit`; the remaining 80 percent uses the configurable parry-versus-dodge split, defaulting to total probabilities of 20 percent hit, 40 percent parry, and 40 percent dodge.
- An incoming weapon-style attack can only be parried with a fully drawn real sword or a held stick. An unsheathing defender has neither available and always dodges unless the passive hit roll occurred.
- Parry, dodge, or hit is chosen on the attack's first tick. Parry cancels the attack's gameplay result, but its attack animation remains active simultaneously with the parry for the original duration; the configurable 1.5-second cooldown begins when that interval ends. Dodge leaves the incoming attack unchanged. A normally completed attack gives the attacker a configurable 1.0-second cooldown.
- During attack cooldown, an agent closer than exactly 50 percent of attack range adds a retreat vector. Each cooldown also receives one deterministic 50-percent strafe roll, one fixed left/right side, and a seeded travel target from 0.5 to 1.0 meters. A successful roll adds a target-orbit tangent only until actual root displacement along that tangent reaches its travel target. Retreat, strafe, and existing tactical steering combine into one normalized stick through the normal mover, so retreat and strafe can occur simultaneously without collision or a second locomotion path.
- In Autonomous mode, a chosen hit remains pending until the attack reaches its authored endpoint. At that endpoint it cancels any still-active defender attack with no cooldown, applies the wound, and applies the selected attack clip's configured stun if the defender remains mobile; a wound that enters Agonising, Passed Out, Crawling, or Dead retains that more severe state. Endpoint hits due on the same tick are collected before effects, so equal-duration simultaneous hits both land; an earlier hit removes a later attack and its pending result.
- Each of the seven sword attacks and nine melee attacks has its own persistent stun duration, all defaulting to 0.5 seconds and quantized to fixed ticks when that attack begins. The appended seventh clip is `pike`; existing six-clip saved settings retain their values and default only `pike`. Stunned agents issue zero speed-stick input while retaining existing mover inertia, cannot launch attacks, and can only choose dodge or hit against a new attack using the normal hit probability; parry is unavailable. When stun ends, an injured-leg agent resumes Slow and an otherwise mobile agent resumes Normal.
- A sword attack against a living grounded target selects only the authored `pike`, `slashRD`, or `slashLD` clips. Other sword clips remain eligible only against standing targets. Grounded hit location remains 80-percent torso and 20-percent head.
- Autonomous hit landing selects all six limbs uniformly for a standing defender. Against any living defender on the floor (`Agonising`, `PassedOut`, or `Crawling`) it selects only torso or head, using a deterministic 80 percent torso / 20 percent head split. Dead defenders are not targeted. This adds no per-agent timer or allocation.
- Every limb has Normal, Injured, and Badly Injured stages. Its existing configurable gauge fills within the current stage and resets to zero on each stage advance; lazy value-plus-last-update-tick decay applies in every stage. A real-sword hit advances exactly one stage immediately, while melee and stick strikes add the configurable 20 points and carry threshold overflow into the next stage.
- One injured leg enters Slow and routes movement through Walk instead of Run. Both injured legs cause three seconds of Agonising before Crawling; torso injury causes ten seconds of Agonising before Crawling; head injury causes thirty seconds Passed Out before Crawling. These three durations are configurable. A badly injured head remains Passed Out and a badly injured torso remains Agonising, permanently preventing crawl. A third full gauge on a badly injured head or torso enters Dead; outer limbs cannot cause death.
- Agonising, Passed Out, Crawling, and Dead agents cannot attack or move. Dead agents immediately zero mover velocity, preserve their final root position and yaw, and use one fixed corpse pose with no animation. A living agent clears a Dead target and issues zero stick input on the next fixed step, then uses the normal bounded left/right head search to reacquire or resolve the post-battle state.
- Authored attacks are filtered by their striking limb: headbutt uses head, hooks/jabs use their named arm, kicks use their named leg, and every weapon attack uses the right arm. An agent with any usable outer limb retains a valid attack. A right-arm injury drops a drawn sword or held stick at its current world location and blocks drawing a sheathed sword.
- In Paired mode, the first-tick defense outcome is still deterministic, but a chosen hit does not land automatically: no cancellation, wound, or stun occurs until Unreal signals the confirmed hit. That future input contract is recorded only in `UNREAL_MIGRATION.md`.
- Live combat and wound-option changes do not restart or advance the run. They are recorded as exact-tick replay inputs; active cooldowns and state timers keep the value assigned when they began.
- Live head-turn-speed changes likewise do not restart or advance the run and replay at their exact input tick.
- Head vision is the only recognition source. A standing enemy first enters persistent recognition memory when its root is inside the current configurable head-centered cone, defaulting to a 180-degree angle and 100-meter range. Proximity never identifies an unseen enemy. A threat is active only while currently visible, or while previously recognized and inside the configurable three-meter retention radius. Dead enemies are removed from recognition.
- Nearest active selection prioritizes recognized threats inside the retention radius, then the nearest currently visible standing enemy. With no active threat, an Attack agent scans its horizontal look once to both `+120` and `-120` degrees; sight reacquires immediately. During that same bounded sweep, an allocation-free fixed bitset remembers every actually visible non-dead enemy.
- Post-battle finishing is strictly gated by the opposing team's standing count: while even one enemy remains standing anywhere, the sweep cannot promote a finishing target. Once no enemy stands, all visible non-dead enemies remembered by the completed sweep become active finishing targets and are selected nearest-first. Dead entries clear from the set; when the set empties, the existing no-target path naturally starts another sweep. A completed sweep with no standing or non-dead visible enemy enters Idle and sheaths the sword while stationary.
- A 20-percent deterministic Wrath choice occurs only when an attacker personally downs its target. Wrath keeps that grounded target for finishing; otherwise the attacker selects its closest active standing threat. A visible incoming attack or any landed hit cancels Wrath and selects the closest active standing threat. Entering Wrath makes the grounded victim emit one full-reach execution scream carrying the executioner's ID. Exactly the closest in-range standing ally of the victim responds, with distance then entity ID as the deterministic tie break. Its existing attack/parry/dodge continues while full Run intent targets the executioner; its head remains on its former target for exactly one second, then targets the executioner. Both non-dead enemies remain active threats for normal containment, and rescue clears if the responder is incapacitated or the executioner is no longer standing.
- Perceived standing targets are assigned load-aware at five hertz, with immediate work when an eligible agent has no valid standing target. Fixed-capacity candidate/order/load buffers first maximize distinct reachable coverage, then distribute surplus attackers with load and distance tie breaking. A valid assignment is committed for a persistent 1.5 seconds by default; attacks and execution rescues remain locked. This target accounting never grants gameplay awareness beyond each agent's active perceived threats.
- Every mobile attacker on a standing target reserves one of eight soft approach sectors. Existing valid reservations remain stable. A new reservation derives a deterministic personal role from the target-to-attacker bearing at acquisition: 25 percent retain center pressure, 50 percent take a left/right side route, and 25 percent take a left/right rear-quarter route. Same-target occupancy can move a reservation to the nearest less-loaded sector. The resulting world bearing remains fixed until retargeting, receives persistent configurable seeded jitter of up to `+/-12` degrees, and uses `+/-0.15` meter approach-radius jitter. Within the persistent configurable 30-meter influence distance, a square-root ramp blends radial pursuit with strong tangential correction; radial input never disappears and grounded finishing bypasses sectors. There is no squad synchronization, exact formation slot, pathfinding, or collision.
- Target relationships are rebuilt into contiguous allocation-free linked groups for diagnostics and same-target ally spacing. Their committed-attacker fields expose intent to the debugger only and never grant gameplay awareness to an agent.
- An agent with at least two active perceived threats enters outnumbered-view steering. It computes the smallest circular arc containing those threats, centers its root-facing target on that arc, and uses a 160-degree tactical view cone. When threats exceed the cone it moves through the open angular gap; attraction to its attack-activation position grows with displacement so retreat cannot continue indefinitely. Attacks, actions, stun, and defense continue to stop stick input under their existing rules.
- Mobile allies sharing one living standing target additionally apply continuous tangential separation when their target-relative bearings are closer than 45 degrees. Every mobile agent also receives soft cross-target separation from standing allies inside the persistent configurable two-meter radius. Both corrections combine with approach steering into one full-speed mover stick and use stable entity-ID tie breaking for exact overlap. They do not create collision, hard exclusion, attack turns, or synchronized formation behavior. Grounded agents exert no separation pressure.
- The core emits deterministic sound events but performs no audio playback. Moving agents pulse on the shared 0.2-second fixed-step clock. Locomotion reach is `maximum reach * clamp(root speed / 5 m/s, 0, 1)`. A real-sword-versus-real-sword parry, the transition into Crawling, and an execution scream on Wrath entry emit one full-maximum-reach event; every parry involving a stick is silent. Maximum reach defaults to three meters and remains configurable, so the current saved live profile may use a larger value.
- Gameplay hearing evaluates only newly emitted events and never walks retained visualization history. Existing locomotion filtering is unchanged. A real-sword clash additionally makes nearby currently Idle agents on both teams investigate the opposing clash participant; it does not directly create recognition, and normal head vision must still see that enemy. Investigation turns the combined head-and-torso horizontal look toward the source within its `+/-120`-degree limit. Failed locomotion investigations retain their deterministic configurable 30-to-40-second non-threat cache.
- Deterministic seed, tick, elapsed time, reset, replay log, exact-tick seek, and return to the live head remain active. A committed seed restarts at tick zero and is persisted for the next launch. Paused position/yaw edits are coalesced to one final replay event per agent per tick; editing a historical tick creates a deterministic branch there. Stick spawn/pickup/drop commands are recorded and branch-trimmed on the same timeline.
