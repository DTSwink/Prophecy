# Right-arm revolution after slashR, September 29

The current kinematic rollout reproduces the reported revolution after upper exit189. The spin is principally right forearm axial rotation relative to the upper arm. It persists with the cone disabled and with upper exit inertia canceled before the first recovery prediction.

Four bounded330-tick captures: current, no_cone, no_inertia, exit_no_inertia. The last is the decisive inertia control: stop at188 after the same final accepted attack pose, cancel inertia after the end callbacks, then allow the first locomotion prediction189. All pre-exit future bone positions through187 match the baseline exactly. The continuously disabled variant still receives the first natural exit prediction before its diagnostic callback and must not be treated as a first-step isolation.

Reconstructed raw upper-checkpoint outputs (`upper_input[90:180] + upper_delta`), including the hand rotation and `ForearmRotationFromHand`, then compared forearm rotation relative to upper arm. In the controlled no-inertia replay, raw reconstruction matches the final NN pose within0.000103degrees over189–229, despite the cone remaining enabled. The reconstructed forearm accumulates approximately364degrees of signed axial rotation over this interval. The no-cone replay's raw output accumulates approximately362degrees. Thus the checkpoint's predicted wrist/arm motion, propagated into forearm roll by the decoder, already contains the revolution; it is not a physical-only response or an inertia-only effect.

`ProphecyNNLocomotionManager.cpp::ForearmRotationFromHand` takes wrist rotation and removes only the swing needed to point elbow-to-hand, retaining roll. Forearm rotation is not an independent upper-checkpoint output. The cone rigidly rotates upper arm, elbow and hand together, so it cannot directly introduce relative forearm twist; its recurrent feedback can change subsequent NN motion. Inertia changes the trajectory but removing it does not eliminate this spin. This case is not a 180degree hand-axis swing singularity: the measured opposing-axis angle stays well below180degrees.

The baseline also reaches a straight elbow around209–217. Pole angles there are undefined/noisy and their apparent accumulated revolution is not reliable evidence; the diagnosis above uses bone quaternions and raw checkpoint reconstruction instead.
